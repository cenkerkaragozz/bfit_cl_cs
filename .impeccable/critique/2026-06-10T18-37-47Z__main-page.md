# Main Page — Conversion + Trust Critique

## Overall Impression

The page has a structurally sound emotional arc. You've solved the hardest part: moving a worried parent from anxiety to aspiration to action across seven sections. The concern selector in HelpSection is genuinely clever UX — it personalizes, pre-qualifies, and carries the selected context all the way to the form. The visual system is cohesive and non-generic.

What's failing is the last mile. After building trust well for 80% of the page, the conversion mechanics collapse right at the finish line: the form CTA is the weakest copy on the entire page, the section that earns the most emotional goodwill (ConfidenceSection, the "it's not laziness" reframe) has zero follow-through, and you promise a WhatsApp fast path in the trust bar without delivering it.

Score: **70/100** (first run — no trend)

## What's Working

1. **Concern selector** — the best UX moment on the page. Taps an anxious parent at their highest-motivation moment, gives immediate personalized response, and carries the selection to the form. No other section does this much trust-building work in so little space.
2. **Concern carry-forward to form** — the hidden `concern` field pre-populating from the parent's selection is smart. Recognition over recall, and it signals that BrainFit listened.
3. **"Tembellik değil" reframe** (ConfidenceSection) — the most emotionally powerful moment on the page. Removing parent guilt is a massive trust builder. The framing "tüm güçleriyle dener ve yorulurlar" is specific and empathic.
4. **Concrete trust chips in Hero** — "45 dakikalık analiz, 16 sayfalık rapor, Kişisel yol haritası" are specific deliverables, not vague promises. A parent can picture what they're getting.
5. **Emotional narrative arc** — Aspiration → Empathy → Reframe → Evidence → Programs → Social proof → Action. The sequence is logically correct.
6. **KVKK mention in form** — a real trust signal for Turkish parents, not decorative compliance theater.
7. **Local proof bars** — Karşıyaka grounding, WhatsApp mention, uzman açıklamalı rapor — makes this feel like a real neighborhood business.
8. **Single conversion goal** — every CTA on the page anchors to `#checkup-form`. No diluted destinations.

## Priority Issues

### P1 — Form submit CTA erases the page's momentum

**Location:** `src/components/CheckUpFormSection.tsx` (submit button)

The final CTA reads "Bilgi Almak İstiyorum" — "I want information." Every previous CTA used action-forward language: "Randevu Al," "Randevusu Alın." The weakest, most passive copy appears at the point of maximum commitment. A parent who has scrolled the entire page, selected their concern, and filled in their name/phone reads "I want information" and their momentum stalls. Fix: align with "Randevusu Al" or move to "Başvur" — something that completes the arc started in the hero.

### P1 — ConfidenceSection earns trust, then drops the thread

**Location:** `src/components/ConfidenceSection.tsx` (both sub-sections)

The "tembellik olmayabilir" reframe is the warmest emotional moment on the page. A parent reading it feels relief and understanding. This is the peak moment where they're most ready to act — and there's no CTA. Not even a subtle link. The emotional current builds to this point and then dissipates into the cognitive carousel. Add a soft CTA immediately after the reframe copy: not a full button, but something like a text link or inline nudge — "Bu noktadan başlayalım →" anchoring to `#checkup-form`.

### P1 — WhatsApp promised, not delivered

**Location:** `src/components/TestimonialSection.tsx` (localProofItems)

The local proof bar says "WhatsApp ile hızlı randevu" but there is no WhatsApp button, link, or `wa.me/` URL anywhere on the page. For a mobile parent (Casey persona), this is a broken promise in the most visible trust section. Either add an actual WhatsApp button — ideally floating on mobile, or inline in the localProofBar — or remove the claim. Promising a fast path and not delivering it actively undermines trust.

### P2 — Programs section appears before social proof

**Location:** `src/components/LandingFunnel.tsx` (section order)

The current order: CheckUpSection → ProgramSection → TestimonialSection. A parent who is still uncertain about BrainFit's credibility is being shown program options (which implies program cost and commitment) before they see any parent testimonials or the expert's photo. Social proof should precede the pitch for what to buy. Swap to: CheckUpSection → TestimonialSection → ProgramSection. Credibility first, then options.

### P2 — Testimonials are too thin to do trust work

**Location:** `src/components/TestimonialSection.tsx`

Rotating quotes: "Zihin Check-Up sonrasında durumu çok daha net gördük." (11 words). "Görüşmemizin sonrasında içimiz çok daha rahatladı." (7 words). Attribution: "BrainFit Karşıyaka Velisi" — completely anonymous. These don't build trust; they're reactions, not stories. A testimonial that builds trust needs: a name (even just "Ahmet'in annesi"), a specific before/after, and a detail only a real parent would know. 7 words from an anonymous source reads as fabricated even when it isn't.

### P2 — %92 stat is unsubstantiated

**Location:** `src/components/CheckUpSection.tsx` (the forest-green stat card)

"%92 BrainFit programlarına katılanların büyük çoğunluğu..." — no source, no sample size, no timeframe, and the qualifier "büyük çoğunluğu" (large majority) within an already-92% stat is internally redundant. For the Riley persona (the skeptical parent who checks things), this stat actively backfires. Replace with something checkable: a number of families served, years in operation, or a Google review count with a live link. Specificity over percentage.

### P2 — Google rating has no verification link

**Location:** `src/components/TestimonialSection.tsx`

"Google Müşteri Puanı: 4.6/5" with star icons and no link. A skeptical parent can't verify this without leaving the page to search manually. A direct link to the Google Business profile turns this from a claim into evidence.

### P3 — CTA label is inconsistent across the page

**Locations:** Hero, HelpSection, CheckUpSection, ProgramSection, CheckUpFormSection

Five different phrasings for one action:
1. "Zihin Check-Up Randevusu Al" (hero)
2. "Bu hedefle randevu al" (HelpSection coral card — generic and unanchored)
3. "Zihin Check-Up Başvurusu" (CheckUpSection — no verb, truncated)
4. "Zihin Check-Up Randevusu Alın" (ProgramSection cards)
5. "Bilgi Almak İstiyorum" (form submit — weakest)

Pick one primary label and use it everywhere except the form submit (which should be action-specific). "Zihin Check-Up Randevusu Al" is the strongest — it names the product and uses an imperative verb.

### P3 — Hero CTA is likely below fold on mobile

**Location:** `src/components/Hero.tsx`

Two paragraphs of body copy sit between the headline and the CTA button. On a 390px mobile viewport, the CTA is likely below the fold after the headline at ~clamp(46px, 11.5vw, 72px) spans 3-4 lines. Consider adding a second, lighter CTA immediately after the headline for mobile, or moving the primary CTA up and letting the copy follow.

### P3 — Concern selector response panel has no micro-CTA

**Location:** `src/components/HelpSection.tsx` (animated response div)

When a parent selects a concern and sees the personalized explanation, they're at their warmest, most engaged moment on the page. The response panel describes what's happening with their child and why — but has no follow-through. "Kaygıyı somut bir başlangıç noktasına çevirelim" is on the static coral card to the left, but it's not connected to the dynamic content. Add a lightweight text link at the bottom of the response copy: something like "Bu profil için Check-Up al →" to capture the warm moment.

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer parent):** The hero asks them to "Al" (take/book) a product they don't yet understand. "Zihin Check-Up Randevusu Al" appears before the CheckUpSection explains what Check-Up is. Jordan's reflex on clicking a CTA for an unknown product is hesitation. No "learn more" secondary path from the hero for curious-but-not-ready parents.

**Riley (Skeptical/Deliberate):** The %92 stat without methodology, the anonymous testimonials, and the unlinked Google rating are three consecutive signals that claims can't be verified. Riley will distrust all three and leave with a credibility deficit exactly where the page should be closing.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile):** CTA probably below fold. "WhatsApp ile hızlı randevu" is a broken promise. The note textarea looks like work at the point where Casey wants to just tap and send.

**Adem (Skeptical Father):** No team credentials visible on the page (who are the specialists?). No pricing transparency (is this expensive? is the Check-Up free?). The empathy-heavy language in ConfidenceSection is correct but risks reading as soft/clinical to a data-oriented father. The "Tıbbi Tanı veya İlaç İçermeyen Bilişsel Yaklaşım" chip is the single strongest trust signal for Adem — make it more prominent.

## Minor Observations

- The hero floating card ("Her çocuğun keşfedilmeyi bekleyen eşsiz bir potansiyeli vardır.") adds warmth but has low information density. Consider replacing with a more specific claim.
- BlogSection appears after the form — good for SEO, but positioned where a converted parent stops reading. No conversion risk.
- The cognitive skill carousel auto-advances every 2.8s. On mobile, the manual tab buttons (8px wide) are very small touch targets (~8×82px). Consider widening to 24px minimum on mobile.
